Automated Induction Soldering Machine

The strongest example of how I work: start with a hard problem, build the risky parts first, and keep going until the mechanical design, controls, and real hardware all line up.

I was asked whether Opto 22 could replace an unreliable hot-air soldering machine with induction heating. That turned into a full machine build: coil development, conveyor design, terminal feeding, pick-and-place, PCB transfer, servo control, wiring, and final integration.

About Me

I’m most interested in the overlap between mechanical design and automation.

I’m a mechanical engineering student at California Baptist University, graduating in May 2027. I’m most drawn to work where I can design a mechanism, make the parts, assemble it, wire it, and then troubleshoot the things that only show up once the system starts moving and operating on its own.

I’ve worked with manual and CNC machining, 3D printing, Inventor, SolidWorks, industrial servo motors, pneumatics, Arduino-based systems, and PLC Control. I’ve become especially interested in automation and electromechanical systems, but more broadly I want to keep working on ambitious mechanical hardware, whether that is robotics, machine design, aerospace, manufacturing, or other systems where strong mechanical design has to perform in the real world.
